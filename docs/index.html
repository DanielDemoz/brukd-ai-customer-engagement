Customer segmentation and engagement

A churn model at 98% that knew nothing about churn

A segmentation, churn and CLV pipeline built on a public shopping dataset, re-checked in October 2026. Both models were scoring their own labels. Here is what the data actually supports.

What the pipeline does

Four Python scripts in scripts/, run in order by run_pipeline.py.

Step 1

Data preparation

Cleans 3,900 shopping records, builds a CLV value and a churn-risk label, and one-hot encodes the categories.

Step 2

Segmentation

K-means groups customers by age, spend, rating and purchase history.

Step 3

Churn model

Random forest and XGBoost classifiers, with SMOTE, trained on the churn-risk label.

Step 4

Recommendations

Rules map each segment and risk level to an engagement action, such as a loyalty offer.

What the re-check found

The earlier README reported a 99.96% CLV model and 87%+ churn accuracy. Both labels were built from the model's own inputs.

The label is a rule: low previous purchases, low rating, no subscription and low spend. A customer with 3 of the 4 is labelled "at risk".

CLV was defined as purchase amount x previous purchases, and both were inputs.

Honest finding: both models re-learned rules they were given. Without the rule inputs, the churn model is no better than always guessing "not at risk", and the CLV model is no better than the average. There is also no date in the data, so a "90-day" churn window cannot be measured.

What to do instead

With this data

  • Use the churn-risk rule directly. It is clear and explainable, and needs no model.
  • Calculate CLV with the formula, and say it is past revenue, not a forecast.
  • Treat segments as a starting point for discussion, not as a finding.

Try the CLV calculator and ROI scenario

With real customer data

  • Define churn from what happened later, for example no purchase in the next 90 days.
  • Keep any field used to build the label out of the model inputs.
  • Hold out a later time period for testing, the way the model would be used.
  • Check results by customer group before using them for offers.

Reliable AI checklist

The six questions of the Reliable AI Adoption Framework, answered for this project.

Business problem

Target retention spend at customers likely to leave. That needs a label based on real future behaviour.

Data readiness

3,900 records from a public shopping-behaviour dataset with no dates. It looks synthetic and cannot support a time-based churn label.

Governance and risk

Leaked labels give confident-looking scores that would misdirect offers. The re-check is part of the project, not a footnote.

Technology and tools

Rules are the honest tool for this data. Models come back once a real label exists.

People and adoption

Marketing staff can read and question a rule. They cannot question a 99.96% score.

Outcomes and monitoring

With real data, compare predicted risk with who actually stopped buying, every quarter.
